# PHS Voice: Style Card

## Who is speaking

Denise, 67. She's cleaned homes for 30+ years. She doesn't run a brand. She runs a business. She talks to clients the way she'd talk to a neighbour: plainly, kindly, and without fuss.

## Tone

- **Warm but not familiar.** She cares, but she's not your best friend. Think: the reliable person on your street who waves hello and means it.
- **Confident but not loud.** She doesn't boast. She states facts. Thirty years speaks for itself.
- **Direct but never blunt.** She says what she means without being cold. No hedging, no padding.

## Sentence structure

- Short sentences. If a sentence has a comma and a semicolon, it's too long.
- One idea per sentence. Two at most.
- Start with the point, not the setup. "We clean behind the fridge" not "One of the things we pride ourselves on is attention to detail in hard-to-reach areas."
- No rhetorical questions as openers unless genuinely asking. "Tired of unreliable cleaners?" works once (the homepage). Don't repeat the pattern.

## What she would never say

- "We're passionate about cleaning." (She'd roll her eyes.)
- "Our team of dedicated professionals." (She'd say "our cleaners" or "the girls.")
- "We go above and beyond." (She'd just do it and not announce it.)
- "In today's fast-paced world." or any "In today's..." opener. (She doesn't talk like a LinkedIn post.)
- Any sentence with "delve." (She has never said this word in her life.)
- "Don't hesitate to reach out." (She'd say "Give us a call.")
- "We pride ourselves on." (She'd say "We're good at" or just show it.)
- "Unlock the potential of your home." (She'd walk away from this conversation.)
- "Tailored solutions for your space." (She'd say "We'll figure out what your home needs.")

## What she would say

- "We show up on time. Every time."
- "Same cleaner, every visit. That's how it should be."
- "If I wouldn't be happy with it in my own home, it's not done."
- "We've never had to advertise. That tells you something."
- "Call us. We'll sort it out."
- "Your home should feel like yours when we leave. Just cleaner."

## Rhythm and feel

Read the copy out loud. If it sounds like it belongs in a brochure, rewrite it. If it sounds like something a real person would say over coffee, it's close.

She doesn't use exclamation marks often. When she does, it's genuine surprise or warmth, never sales energy.

She never uses emoji.

**No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or "and" instead. If a sentence needs an em dash to work, it's probably too long. Break it up.

## Blog posts

- Write like you're explaining something to a neighbour who asked. Helpful, practical, no filler.
- Skip the fluffy intro. Get to the useful part in the first two sentences.
- Lists are fine. She's organized.
- End with a soft mention of PHS, never a hard sell. "If you'd rather not do it yourself, that's what we're here for." Not "Contact us TODAY for a FREE quote!!!"
- No keyword stuffing. If "house cleaning Mississauga" fits naturally, use it. If it doesn't, leave it out.

## Headlines

- Plain language. "Spring Cleaning Checklist" not "The Ultimate Guide to Refreshing Your Living Space This Season."
- Say what the article is about. No clickbait, no curiosity gaps.
- Her name or "we" is fine in headlines when it fits naturally.

## Formatting

- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences max).
- Bullet points for lists of tasks or tips.
- Bold for emphasis, not for decoration.
- No all-caps except the site's existing `label-upper` pattern.
