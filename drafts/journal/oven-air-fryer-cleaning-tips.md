Baked-on grease in an oven and burnt residue in an air fryer are two of the most common things I see in kitchens. They're also two of the things people try the most unusual fixes on.

Right now there are videos everywhere showing clever tricks with dish soap, baking soda, and water baths. Some of them are genuinely useful. Others will leave you with a warped basket or a coating you can't reverse.

Here's what I've seen work, and what to skip.

## Cleaning Your Oven

**Baking soda and water paste**: This is the one that actually holds up. Mix baking soda with just enough water to make a paste, spread it over the interior, and leave it overnight. Wipe it out in the morning. It lifts grease without scratching the enamel.

**Vinegar spray after the paste**: Once you've wiped out the baking soda, a light spray of white vinegar helps cut any residue left behind. It also neutralizes the smell.

**The self-clean cycle**: Use it carefully. The self-clean function runs at very high heat. It works, but it puts stress on the oven. If your oven is older or has a faulty seal, it can cause problems. I'd use it once or twice a year at most.

**Dish soap in a hot water bowl**: You'll see this one on TikTok. The idea is that steam from boiling water in the oven loosens grease. It does loosen some surface grime, but it doesn't cut through heavy buildup. It's a light-maintenance step, not a deep clean.

**Oven cleaner sprays**: These work, but they're harsh. Make sure the kitchen is well ventilated. Don't use them on self-cleaning ovens, as the coating inside can be damaged by the chemicals. Read the label for your specific model before you spray anything.

What I'd avoid: anything abrasive on the glass door. Steel wool and rough scrubbing pads scratch it. Once the glass is scratched, it's scratched for good.

## Cleaning Your Air Fryer

Air fryers have become standard in most kitchens I work in. They also collect grease faster than people expect.

**Warm soapy water after every use**: This is the only reliable method. Let the basket cool, wash it with dish soap and warm water, and dry it fully before putting it back. A soft brush helps get into the mesh.

**Baking soda paste on stubborn spots**: Same principle as the oven. Apply it, let it sit 15 to 20 minutes, then scrub gently with a soft brush or cloth.

**The water and dish soap bath trick**: Some videos suggest filling the basket with water and dish soap, then running the air fryer for a few minutes. It can loosen grease on the interior walls. I'd say it helps, but it's not a substitute for hand washing the basket.

What I'd avoid: the dishwasher, unless your manual specifically says it's safe. High heat in a dishwasher warps some baskets and strips non-stick coatings. Check your manual once and follow it every time.

Also avoid soaking the main unit. The basket is removable for a reason. Water near the heating element causes damage that's often not worth repairing.

## A Note on Non-Stick Coatings

Both ovens and air fryers often have non-stick surfaces. Once that coating is scratched or flaking, the appliance is harder to clean and may need replacing sooner. The single best thing you can do is use soft cloths and brushes only. Never metal. Never rough green scrubbing pads.

It takes a few extra minutes to be gentle. It saves the appliance.

## When Grease Has Built Up for Too Long

If an oven or air fryer hasn't been cleaned in months, one round of baking soda paste won't do it. It usually takes two or three applications over a couple of days. That's normal. Don't push harder with more abrasive tools. Be patient and repeat the gentle method.
