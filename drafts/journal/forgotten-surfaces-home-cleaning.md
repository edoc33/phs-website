Most people clean what they can see. The counter, the toilet, the kitchen floor. But there are surfaces in every home that get skipped week after week, sometimes for years. They're not hidden. You've just stopped registering them.

Here are the ones I find in nearly every home I walk into.

## The Top of the Refrigerator

Almost nobody looks up there. Grease from cooking floats up and sticks, then dust settles on top of the grease. After a few months it becomes a thick, grey film that takes some effort to remove. Wipe it down with a cloth dampened with warm soapy water. You may need to go over it twice. Do it every couple of months and it stays manageable.

## Inside the Microwave Door Seal

People wipe the inside of the microwave. Almost nobody wipes the rubber gasket around the door. Food splatter gets in there and stays. It can get mouldy. Run a damp cloth along the seal every few weeks. It takes thirty seconds.

## The Dishwasher Filter

Your dishwasher cleans your dishes, but the filter at the bottom collects food debris and never cleans itself. Pull it out, rinse it under the tap, and scrub it gently with an old toothbrush. Do this once a month. If it smells off when you open the door, the filter is usually why.

## Underneath Furniture You Don't Move Often

Sofas, beds, dressers. Dust and debris collect underneath them and most people never see it unless they're moving. This matters more than it sounds. Dust mites live in that layer. Pet hair compacts under there. If anyone in your home has allergies, this is one of the first places to address. Pull furniture away from walls a few times a year and clean underneath.

## Light Switches and Door Handles

These are touched dozens of times a day and rarely wiped down. A damp cloth with a small amount of dish soap takes care of them. Do the plates around the switches too, not just the switch itself. Same goes for door handles and the area around them where hands naturally push.

## The Bathroom Exhaust Fan Cover

Bathroom fans pull moisture and steam through a vent cover. That cover collects dust, and when enough builds up, the fan can't do its job. Take the cover off, wash it in soapy water, let it dry, and put it back. Check it every few months.

## Window Tracks

The grooves that your windows slide in collect dirt, dead bugs, and grime. A dry brush or old toothbrush loosens it. Then a damp cloth or cotton swab finishes the job. This is one of those things that once you do it, you can't believe you left it so long.

## The Tops of Door Frames

This one surprises people. Dust settles on the top of every door frame in your home. You won't see it unless you're tall or you look for it. A damp cloth on top of the frame once every month or two keeps it from building into a layer.

## Behind the Toilet

Most people clean the bowl and the outside of the tank. The wall and floor directly behind the toilet get ignored. Dust collects there, and it's close enough to the bowl that you want it clean. Get a cloth behind there when you do the rest of the bathroom.

## The Washing Machine Drum

Washing machines can get mouldy, especially front-loaders. The rubber gasket around the door is the worst spot. Wipe it dry after each wash and leave the door open slightly so air can get in. Once a month, run an empty hot cycle with a cup of white vinegar to clean the drum.
