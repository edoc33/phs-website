Wildfire smoke isn't just unpleasant to breathe. It leaves fine particles on surfaces, in fabrics, and inside your HVAC system. When an air quality advisory lifts, the smoke residue in your home doesn't disappear with it.

Here's what to focus on.

## Change Your Furnace Filter First

This is the most important step. Your furnace or central air system was pulling air through the house during the smoke event. The filter will be loaded with fine particles.

Put in a new filter before you do anything else. If you run the system with a clogged filter, it redistributes whatever it caught. A MERV-11 or higher filter is worth it if you don't already use one.

## Wipe Down Hard Surfaces

Smoke particles are tiny and they settle on everything. Countertops, shelves, windowsills, the tops of appliances, the blades of ceiling fans. These all need a wipe with a damp microfibre cloth.

Start high and work down. Wipe ceiling fan blades before you do the counters below them.

For stubborn smoke film, a small amount of white vinegar diluted in water works on most hard surfaces. Avoid it on natural stone (see note below).

**Natural stone countertops**: Use warm water and a few drops of pH-neutral dish soap instead. Vinegar will etch marble and travertine.

## Wash What You Can Wash

Soft surfaces hold smoke odour longer than hard ones. If you had windows open during the advisory, this matters more.

- **Curtains and drapes**: Wash or steam-clean them. If they're dry-clean only, air them outside on a day with good air quality.
- **Bedding and pillowcases**: Wash on a warm cycle.
- **Throw cushion covers**: Remove and wash if possible.
- **Upholstered furniture**: Sprinkle baking soda on fabric surfaces, leave it for 20 to 30 minutes, then vacuum it up. This helps pull odour out.

## Vacuum Thoroughly, Including Corners

Fine particles settle into carpets and area rugs. Vacuum slowly, especially in corners and along baseboards where particles collect.

Use a vacuum with a HEPA filter if you have one. A regular vacuum without a sealed filter can redistribute fine particles back into the air.

After vacuuming, change or empty the vacuum bag or canister outside.

## Don't Forget These Spots

Most people wipe the obvious surfaces and stop there. These areas get missed:

- **Window tracks and screens**: Screens filter some of what comes in. Wipe them with a damp cloth or rinse outside.
- **Blinds**: Each slat collects dust and smoke film. Wipe them one by one with a damp cloth.
- **Light fixtures and ceiling fans**: Smoke rises and settles on anything near the ceiling.
- **Air vents and returns**: Wipe the grilles with a damp cloth.

## The Smell That Won't Leave

If the smoke odour is still noticeable after cleaning, it's usually coming from soft furnishings or from inside the HVAC system. A bowl of white vinegar or activated charcoal set out overnight can help absorb odour in a room. These aren't miracle fixes, but they help.

If the smell is coming through the vents, you may need a professional duct cleaning. One smoke event won't usually require it, but if your home smells like smoke every time the furnace runs, that's the likely cause.
