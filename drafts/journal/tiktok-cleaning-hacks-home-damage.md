A few viral cleaning tricks are making the rounds again. Some of them work. Some of them will quietly ruin things in your home that cost a lot to fix.

I've been cleaning homes in Toronto and Mississauga for over 30 years. I've seen the aftermath of a few of these hacks. Here's what I'd tell a neighbour.

## The Ones That Can Actually Hurt Your Home

**Dryer sheets on baseboards**: People swear this repels dust. It does leave a residue, though. Over time that residue attracts more dust and can dull painted surfaces. A dry microfibre cloth does the same job without the buildup.

**Vinegar on natural stone or grout**: Vinegar is acidic. It will etch marble, limestone, and travertine counters. It also breaks down grout sealer over time. If your counters or bathroom tile are natural stone, skip the vinegar entirely. Use a pH-neutral cleaner.

**Baking soda paste on stainless steel appliances**: Baking soda is mildly abrasive. It can leave fine scratches on stainless finishes, especially if you scrub against the grain. Use a damp microfibre cloth and go with the grain. That's it.

**The "slow vacuuming" trick for carpet**: This one is real, actually. Moving slowly over carpet does give the vacuum more time to pull debris up from the pile. Just don't overdo the passes. Running over the same strip six or seven times won't help more, and on older carpets it adds wear.

**Hydrogen peroxide on coloured grout**: Some people use this to brighten grout lines. It works on white grout. On coloured or grey grout, it can bleach unevenly and leave patchy lines. Test a hidden spot first, or just don't bother.

**Scrubbing tiles with steel wool or abrasive pads**: I see this one come up for hard water stains in bathrooms. Steel wool will scratch ceramic and porcelain tile. Once the glaze is scratched, dirt catches in those marks and the tile never looks clean again. Use a nylon scrubbing pad instead.

**Putting cast iron or wooden cutting boards in the dishwasher**: This comes up in kitchen cleaning posts. Cast iron will rust. Wooden boards will crack and split. Hand wash both. Dry immediately.

**Mixing bleach with vinegar or hydrogen peroxide**: This one isn't just a home damage issue. It produces chlorine gas. It's genuinely dangerous. Never mix bleach with anything except water.

## What Actually Works

Most of what I use daily is not complicated.

**Microfibre cloths**: These pick up dust and bacteria without chemicals. Wash them separately in hot water with no fabric softener.

**Dish soap and warm water**: Handles most surfaces, including painted walls, cabinet fronts, and everyday counters.

**A proper grout brush**: A stiff-bristled brush with a pH-neutral cleaner does more for grout than most viral "miracle" products.

**Regular vacuuming with a clean filter**: This matters more than any hack. A clogged filter means your vacuum is just pushing dust around.

## Why These Hacks Keep Spreading

Short videos can't show you the damage that happens six months later. A before-and-after clip of shiny marble after a vinegar scrub looks great. The etching shows up slowly. By then, the video is long forgotten.

I'm not against trying new things. But if something seems too quick and easy for a surface you paid good money for, it's worth a second look before you start scrubbing.
