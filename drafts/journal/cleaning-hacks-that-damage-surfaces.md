Not every cleaning trick you see online is wrong. Some are genuinely useful. But a few of the popular ones cause real damage, and I've spent years fixing what they leave behind.

Here's what I'd steer you away from, and why.

## Vinegar on natural stone

White vinegar is acidic. Natural stone, including marble, travertine, and limestone, is not acid-safe. Vinegar etches the surface. It looks like a dull cloudy patch that doesn't wipe off because it isn't a film. It's damage to the stone itself. Use a pH-neutral cleaner on stone surfaces, or just warm water and a soft cloth.

This matters in a lot of Toronto homes. Marble vanities and travertine tiles are common in condos and renovated older houses.

## Dish soap on hardwood floors

A little dish soap in a bucket seems harmless. It isn't. Dish soap leaves a residue that builds up over time and makes floors look hazy. It can also break down the finish if used regularly. Use a cleaner made specifically for hardwood, applied sparingly with a damp, not wet, mop.

## Baking soda paste on chrome or stainless

Baking soda is mildly abrasive. On chrome fixtures or stainless steel, scrubbing with a paste can leave fine scratches that dull the finish over time. Those scratches also give grime more to hold onto. For chrome and stainless, a microfibre cloth with a bit of dish soap is usually enough.

## Bleach on grout

This one is common and the results look good at first. But bleach weakens grout over time. It strips the colour and breaks down the material. Repeated use can cause grout to crumble. For most grout, a stiff brush with warm water and dish soap handles surface grime. For deeper staining, a oxygen-based cleaner made for grout is safer.

## The "scrub it harder" approach to stains

Scrubbing a stain aggressively pushes it deeper into carpet or fabric fibres. Blotting works better. Press, lift, press, lift. Work from the outside of the stain toward the centre. More pressure is rarely the answer.

## Dryer sheets on wood furniture

Some people rub dryer sheets on wood furniture to reduce dust. The coating leaves a waxy film that builds up. It can make furniture look smudged and is hard to remove later. A dry microfibre cloth picks up dust without leaving anything behind.

## Steam cleaners on hardwood or laminate

Steam machines work well on tile and grout. On hardwood or laminate, the moisture gets into the seams between boards. It can cause swelling, warping, and lifting over time. Check the manufacturer's guidance before using a steam cleaner on any floor that isn't solid tile or stone.
