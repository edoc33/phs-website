Your toilet gets scrubbed regularly. Your kitchen sink, probably not as often. Ontario microbiologists recently flagged the kitchen sink as the germiest spot in most homes, and honestly, after 30 years of cleaning kitchens, I'm not surprised.

The sink collects raw meat juices, food scraps, dirty dish water, and sitting moisture all day. The drain, the basin, and the faucet handles are all touched constantly, and rarely cleaned properly. Here's how to actually deal with it.

## The Parts People Miss

Most people wipe the basin occasionally. That's not enough.

**The drain and strainer**: Food and grease collect here and bacteria grow fast in the warmth and moisture. Pull the strainer out weekly and scrub it with an old toothbrush and dish soap.

**The faucet handles**: These get touched with raw-chicken hands, wet hands, and dirty hands all day. Wipe them down with a damp cloth and a bit of dish soap every couple of days. Don't forget the base where the handle meets the counter.

**The faucet aerator**: That small screen at the tip of the faucet. Unscrew it once a month and rinse out the buildup. Mineral deposits and grime collect there, especially with Toronto's hard water.

**Under the lip of the basin**: The underside edge of the sink, right where it meets the counter or the rim. This spot stays damp and gets ignored. Wipe it every time you clean the basin.

**The sponge**: If you're using a sponge to wipe the sink, it may be spreading bacteria around rather than removing it. Swap it for a cloth you can wash, or replace sponges every week.

## How to Actually Clean the Sink

You don't need anything fancy. Here's what works.

1. Clear everything out of and around the sink.
2. Sprinkle a bit of baking soda in the basin.
3. Scrub with a damp cloth or scrubbing pad, including the sides and the drain area.
4. Rinse well.
5. Wipe down the faucet handles and base.
6. Dry the basin with a clean cloth. Leaving it wet is part of the problem.

For stainless steel sinks, dry it after every use if you can. Standing water leads to water spots and speeds up the grime cycle. For porcelain, the same applies. Porcelain stains more easily and a quick dry goes a long way.

If your drain smells, pour a cup of baking soda down it, follow with a cup of white vinegar, let it fizz for a few minutes, then flush with hot water. Do this once a week. It won't fix a blocked drain, but it keeps everyday odour from building up.

## How Often Should You Clean the Sink?

A quick wipe-down with soap and water should happen daily, or at least after preparing raw meat or fish. A proper scrub of the basin, drain strainer, and faucet should happen once or twice a week. The aerator and the underside of the rim can be monthly.

It sounds like more than it is. Once you're in the habit, the whole thing takes three minutes.

## One More Thing

If you have a garbage disposal, run it with cold water every time you use it, and don't let food sit in it. Once a week, put a few ice cubes and a bit of coarse salt in and run it to help scrub the inside. A cut lemon helps with smell.

The sink is one of those things that looks clean when it isn't. Giving it proper attention a few times a week makes a real difference, not just for appearance but for what's actually living on the surface.
