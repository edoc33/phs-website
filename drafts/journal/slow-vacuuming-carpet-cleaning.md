Most people vacuum too fast. It's understandable. Vacuuming feels like a chore you want to get through. But moving the vacuum quickly across carpet means the suction barely has time to pull anything up. You're mostly just pushing the machine back and forth.

Slowing down is one of the simplest things you can do to actually clean your floors instead of just tidying the surface.

## Why speed matters

Carpet fibres trap dust, pet hair, skin cells, and grit between them. Suction needs a moment to work through those fibres and pull the debris up. When you move fast, you get surface dirt but leave the rest behind.

A slower pass gives the vacuum time to do its job. That's it. No special equipment needed.

## How slow is slow enough

You're aiming for roughly two to three seconds per foot of carpet. That's slower than you're probably used to. Try it once and you'll notice how deliberate it feels compared to your normal pace.

On your forward stroke, go slow. The return stroke can be a bit quicker. Most of the work happens going in.

## Overlap your passes

Another thing most people skip. Overlapping each pass by about half the vacuum head width means you cover every inch twice. Dust and hair don't line up neatly with your vacuum's path, so a single pass always misses something.

This matters most near walls and furniture edges, where debris accumulates and gets pushed around rather than picked up.

## High-traffic areas need more attention

**Front hall and entryway**: This is where grit, salt residue, and outdoor debris concentrate. In September in Toronto, you're starting to see that again as people come in from wet and muddy conditions. Go over it multiple times.
**Living room and family room**: Pet hair and fine dust settle here. These areas often look clean but aren't.
**Under furniture**: Move it when you can. Dust builds up faster under couches and beds than most people expect.

## HEPA filters make a real difference

If your vacuum has a HEPA filter, make sure it's clean. A clogged filter reduces suction and sends fine particles back into the air. Check it every few uses.

If your vacuum doesn't have one and you or someone in your home has allergies or respiratory issues, it's worth considering when you next replace the machine. Fall is a good time for this, as windows start closing up and indoor air quality matters more.

## What slow vacuuming won't fix

Deep-set stains and ground-in grit that's been there for a long time won't come out with better vacuuming technique alone. At some point, carpet needs a deeper clean. That might be a rental steam cleaner, a professional carpet clean, or in some cases, an honest look at whether the carpet has reached the end of its useful life.

For everything else, slow down. You'll use the same amount of time if you cover less ground per pass, but you'll actually clean the floor.
