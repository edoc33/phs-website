The idea is simple: move the vacuum slower and it picks up more. It's been making the rounds online, and people are genuinely curious whether they've been vacuuming wrong this whole time.

The short answer is yes, pace matters. But it's one of several things that make a real difference, and it's not the most important one.

## Why Speed Actually Matters

Most vacuums work by creating suction combined with brush roll agitation. When you push too fast, the brush doesn't have enough time to loosen debris from the carpet fibres before the suction head passes over. You pick up surface dust but miss what's buried deeper.

A slower pass, roughly half the speed most people use, gives the brush roll time to do its job. This is especially true for carpets with a thicker pile, and for pet hair, which tangles into fibres and resists a quick pass.

One slow forward stroke is worth more than three fast ones.

## The Direction You Vacuum Matters Too

Most people vacuum in one direction. Going back and forth in the same line on the same angle means you're only lifting fibres one way. Change direction. Vacuum in rows going one way, then go over the same area going the other way, or at a diagonal. It sounds fussy, but it genuinely loosens more.

This matters most in high-traffic areas, hallways, and anywhere a pet sleeps regularly.

## What Else People Miss

**The pre-vacuum step**: If there's debris, crumbs, or anything visible on the carpet, pick it up by hand first. Vacuuming over large pieces strains the motor and often just scatters them.

**Changing the bag or emptying the canister**: A vacuum that's more than half full loses suction noticeably. Most people wait too long. Empty it when it reaches the halfway mark.

**The brush roll itself**: Check it every month or so. Hair and thread wrap around the brush and reduce how well it agitates the carpet. Most vacuums let you cut and pull this out easily. It takes two minutes and makes a big difference.

**Vacuuming too rarely**: Once a week is the minimum for most homes. If you have pets or kids, twice a week in main living areas keeps allergens from building up. Carpets hold dust mites, pollen, and dander between the fibres. You can't see it, but it's there.

**Edges and corners**: The main head doesn't reach the last inch along walls and in corners. Use the crevice tool. These spots collect a surprising amount of dust and hair, and they're easy to skip every single pass.

## When Vacuuming Isn't Enough

Slow vacuuming and good technique help a lot, but they don't replace a deep clean. Over time, grit and fine particles work their way down to the base of the carpet fibre where no vacuum reaches. This is what makes older carpets look dull and feel rough underfoot.

Most carpets benefit from a professional deep clean once a year. Twice a year if you have pets. In Toronto and Mississauga, the combination of winter salt, wet boots, and dry indoor air means carpets take a harder beating than people realize.

Between deep cleans, a slow, deliberate vacuuming routine is genuinely the best thing you can do. The technique isn't complicated. It just takes a little more time than most people give it.
