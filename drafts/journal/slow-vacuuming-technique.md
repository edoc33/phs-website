There is a cleaning tip going around that says you should vacuum your carpets very slowly, as slowly as one foot per second, to pick up significantly more dirt. The claim is that most people rush and leave most of the debris behind.

There is some truth to it. But the full picture is more useful.

## The Slow Vacuuming Claim

The idea is that a slower pass gives the vacuum's suction more time to pull debris up from the carpet fibres. A few published tests have shown that slower passes do pick up more fine particles, particularly dust and allergens.

This matters in June. Windows are open. Pollen is coming in. Dust settles into carpet and rugs faster than most people realize.

## What the Research Actually Shows

Slower vacuuming does help, but only up to a point. Once you are moving at a reasonable pace, going even slower gives diminishing returns. The bigger factors are:

**How often you vacuum**: Once a week is the minimum for most homes. Twice a week if you have pets or kids.

**The condition of your vacuum's filter**: A clogged filter kills suction. Clean or replace it regularly. If you notice the vacuum sounds the same but is picking up less, the filter is often the reason.

**Whether you overlap your passes**: Go over each strip twice, once forward, once back. This is more effective than going very slowly in one direction.

**The direction you vacuum**: In high-traffic areas, try alternating the direction each session. Carpet fibres get compressed in one direction. Changing it helps lift them back up.

**The height setting on the vacuum head**: Many vacuums have a pile height adjustment. If it is set too low on a thick carpet, the head drags and the airflow gets blocked. Set it correctly for the carpet you are on.

## Rugs Over Hardwood

This comes up a lot in Toronto homes, especially older ones. Area rugs over hardwood are common, and they need a bit more attention.

Shake smaller rugs out the door or over a railing before vacuuming. You will be surprised what comes out. For larger area rugs, vacuum both sides twice a year. Dirt works its way through the rug and sits between the rug and the floor underneath. Left there long enough, it acts like sandpaper on your hardwood.

## Hard Floors Are Not Vacuum-Proof

A lot of people skip vacuuming hard floors and go straight to mopping. That is a mistake. Mopping dry debris just moves it around and can scratch the surface. Vacuum or sweep first, then mop.

Use a hard floor attachment if your vacuum has one. The spinning brush roll on a standard head can scratch hardwood and vinyl. Turn it off or switch the head.

## The One Thing That Matters More Than Technique

Whatever technique you use, the single most important thing is doing it consistently. A slow, thorough vacuum once a month is not better than a quick one done twice a week.

Dirt, pollen, and pet hair that sits in carpet fibres for weeks gets ground deeper with every footstep. The longer it sits, the harder it is to remove.

## A Practical Routine

**High-traffic areas** (hallways, kitchen mats, living room): Two or three times a week.

**Bedrooms**: Once a week.

**Under furniture**: Once a month, or more if you have pets.

**Mattresses**: Once a month with the upholstery attachment. More people should do this and most do not.

The slow vacuuming tip is not wrong. But it is one small piece. The technique matters less than showing up regularly and doing it properly.
