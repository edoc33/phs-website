An oven that hasn't been cleaned in a while doesn't just look bad. It smokes every time you use it, it affects how food tastes, and it makes your kitchen smell like old grease. Fall is when ovens start working hard again, so now is a good time to deal with it before Thanksgiving.

Here's what actually works.

## Take Everything Out First

Remove the racks and the bottom tray if you have one. These are much easier to clean outside the oven. Soak them in hot soapy water in your sink or bathtub for an hour. The grease loosens on its own and scrubs off without much effort.

## For the Inside of the Oven

Skip the commercial oven spray if you can. The fumes are harsh and you need to air out the kitchen for a long time after. A simple baking soda paste works well and doesn't require ventilation warnings.

**Make the paste**: Mix about half a cup of baking soda with enough water to form a thick spreadable paste.

**Apply it**: Spread it over the inside of the oven, avoiding the heating elements. Cover the door glass, the walls, and the bottom. It will look yellow-brown where it hits old grease. That's normal.

**Wait**: Leave it for at least four hours. Overnight is better. The paste breaks down the grease without you having to scrub hard.

**Scrub and wipe**: Use a damp cloth or a non-scratch scrubbing pad. Work in sections. Wipe the paste away with a clean damp cloth. You may need to rinse the cloth several times.

**Finish with vinegar**: Spray a little white vinegar over any leftover baking soda residue. It will fizz briefly and wipe away clean.

## The Door Glass

This is usually the most satisfying part because you can actually see the result. Apply the same baking soda paste, leave it for twenty minutes, then scrub gently with a non-scratch pad. Wipe clean. If there's grease between the glass panels, that's a different job and may require removing the door, which depends on your oven model.

## What to Skip

**Self-clean mode**: It gets the oven very hot and burns everything off, which sounds convenient. But it can damage oven seals, trip your smoke detector, and leave a fine ash you still have to wipe out. In older ovens it can also wear out the heating elements faster. It's not something we recommend using regularly.

**Wire brushes or steel wool**: These scratch the oven interior and can damage the finish. A non-scratch pad is enough if the paste has done its job.

## How Often Should You Clean It

If you cook regularly, once every three months keeps things manageable. If you only use it a few times a week and catch spills when they happen, twice a year is fine. The longer you wait, the harder the job.

A quick wipe of the bottom after something spills, while the oven is still slightly warm but not hot, prevents most of the baked-on buildup in the first place.

## When It's Beyond a DIY Job

Some ovens, especially ones that haven't been cleaned in years, have layers of carbonized grease that baking soda won't fully address. If you've tried the method above and it's still not right, or if you simply don't want to spend your weekend on it, that's a reasonable call.
