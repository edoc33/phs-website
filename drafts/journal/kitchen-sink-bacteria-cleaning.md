Ontario microbiologists recently told the Toronto Star that the kitchen sink carries more bacteria than the average toilet seat. Most people are surprised by that. They shouldn't be.

Think about what goes into your sink every day. Raw chicken juice. Dish rags that sit wet for hours. Food scraps that collect around the drain. The sink gets rinsed constantly but rarely scrubbed. Rinsing is not cleaning.

## Why the Kitchen Sink Gets So Dirty

Bacteria thrive in moist, food-rich environments. Your sink is both. The drain area and the rubber gasket around it (if you have a garburator) are especially bad. So is the sponge sitting in the corner of the sink. That sponge may be the single dirtiest object in your home.

The faucet handle is another one people miss. You touch it with dirty hands before washing, and then again after. It almost never gets scrubbed.

## How to Actually Clean Your Kitchen Sink

This does not need to be complicated. A few minutes, done regularly, makes a real difference.

**The basin**: Scrub it with dish soap and a brush, not a sponge. Get into the corners. Rinse well. Do this every day or two, not just when it looks dirty.

**The drain area**: Pour a kettle of boiling water down the drain once a week. It won't sterilize anything, but it flushes out the grease and food buildup that bacteria feed on.

**The garburator gasket**: Lift it out if you can. Scrub both sides with an old toothbrush and dish soap. Rinse it. Put it back. Most people have never done this. It tends to be alarming the first time.

**The faucet and handles**: Wipe them down with a damp cloth and a small amount of dish soap. Do this when you clean the basin. It takes ten seconds.

**The sponge**: Replace it every one to two weeks. In between, wring it out completely after every use and let it dry somewhere with airflow. A wet sponge left flat on the sink edge is a bacteria hotel.

**Dish rags**: Wash them in hot water at least twice a week. If they smell sour before wash day, that's already too long.

## What About Disinfecting?

You don't need a cabinet full of sprays. A diluted bleach solution (one tablespoon of bleach to one litre of water) works well on stainless steel. Let it sit for a minute, then rinse. Don't mix bleach with anything else, including vinegar. That combination produces fumes you don't want in your kitchen.

For composite or porcelain sinks, check what the manufacturer recommends. Some materials scratch or discolour with bleach.

## The Deeper Clean

Even if you keep up with the daily scrub, a thorough kitchen cleaning a few times a year makes sense. That means pulling out the dish rack, cleaning under it, scrubbing the sink surround, and getting behind the faucet where grime collects. It also means wiping down the cabinet under the sink, which is often overlooked and sometimes shows early signs of a slow leak.
