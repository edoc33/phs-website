Ontario microbiologists recently confirmed what most professional cleaners already suspect: the kitchen sink is one of the dirtiest spots in the average home. Not the toilet. Not the garbage can. The sink.

It makes sense when you think about it. Raw meat juices, vegetable soil, egg residue, standing water, a damp sponge sitting in the corner. Everything bacteria need to thrive ends up in that basin, usually multiple times a day.

Here is what to do about it.

## The sink basin itself

Rinse the basin after every use. That sounds obvious, but most people rinse dishes and leave food residue sitting on the stainless steel or porcelain.

Once a day, scrub the basin with dish soap and a brush. Not a sponge. Sponges stay wet and become a bacteria farm faster than almost anything else in the kitchen.

Once a week, pour a small amount of undiluted white vinegar over the basin and let it sit for a few minutes before rinsing. This handles mineral buildup and keeps odours down.

## The drain and drain stopper

The drain is where things get genuinely unpleasant. Biofilm builds up inside the drain opening and along the stopper faster than most people realise.

Pull the stopper out weekly. Scrub it with an old toothbrush and dish soap. Rinse it clean before putting it back.

For the drain itself, pour boiling water down it once a week. Follow that with a cup of baking soda, wait ten minutes, then chase it with white vinegar. It fizzes, which feels satisfying, and it does actually help with the odour and light buildup.

## The faucet and handles

Faucet handles are touched constantly, often with dirty hands. They need more attention than most people give them.

Wipe them down daily with a damp cloth and a drop of dish soap. Get into the base of the faucet where grime collects. If you have chrome fixtures, dry them after wiping so water spots do not set.

## The sponge problem

If you are using a sponge to clean your sink, you are spreading bacteria more than removing it. A sponge stays damp between uses and picks up everything it touches.

Switch to a brush with a handle. It dries faster, is easier to rinse clean, and does not hold onto food particles the same way. Replace it every four to six weeks.

If you will not give up your sponge, at least wring it out completely after every use and let it dry on a rack, not flat on the counter or in the sink.

## The dish rack and mat

This one gets overlooked entirely. The dish rack sits in a puddle of water for most of the day. Mold and mildew grow on the rubber mat underneath before most people notice anything is wrong.

Pull the rack and mat out weekly. Scrub both with dish soap and rinse them well. Let them dry fully before putting them back.

## A quick weekly reset

If you want a simple routine, here is what works:

- **Daily wipe**: Rinse basin after each use, wipe faucet handles with a damp cloth
- **Dish brush swap**: Use a brush instead of a sponge, rinse it clean each time
- **Weekly scrub**: Basin, stopper, faucet base, dish rack, and mat
- **Weekly drain flush**: Boiling water, then baking soda and vinegar
- **Monthly deep look**: Check under the rack, around the base of the faucet, and the caulk line where the sink meets the counter

That caulk line is worth paying attention to. If it has gone grey or is pulling away from the sink, mold is likely living in the gap. A toothbrush and some diluted bleach can handle early-stage mold there, but if it has been ignored for a while, it may need resealing.

The kitchen sink is one of those spots that looks clean because water runs through it all day. That running water is not cleaning it. It is adding to the moisture that makes bacteria and mold comfortable.
