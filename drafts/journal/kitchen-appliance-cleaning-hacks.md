Cleaning hacks for small appliances are all over TikTok right now, and people are trying them. Some are genuinely useful. Others have caused damage, and at least one viral method reportedly broke a fan. After cleaning homes for 30 years, I've seen what happens when the wrong technique meets the wrong surface. Here's my take on the ones showing up most often.

## Air Fryer: The Water and Dish Soap Soak

The hack: fill the basket with soapy water, run the air fryer for a few minutes to loosen grease, then wipe clean.

**What actually works**: Soaking the basket and tray in hot soapy water in your sink. Let it sit for 10 to 15 minutes, scrub with a non-abrasive sponge, rinse, and dry completely before putting it back. That's it. It works every time.

**What to avoid**: Running water inside the appliance itself. The heating element is not sealed against moisture in most models. Water can get into places it shouldn't, and you risk damaging the unit or creating a safety issue.

**The drawer and interior walls**: Wipe these with a damp cloth after every few uses. Grease builds up fast and bakes on harder each time. A bit of baking soda on a damp cloth cuts through it without scratching.

## Ceiling Fan: The Pillowcase Method

The hack: slip an old pillowcase over each blade, pull it back slowly to catch the dust instead of spreading it.

This one is actually good. It works. The dust goes into the pillowcase instead of onto your floor and furniture. Shake the pillowcase outside, then wash it.

**What to avoid**: Spraying cleaning products directly onto the blades while the fan is mounted. One viral method involved spraying and wiping with too much moisture around the motor housing. That's how you get into trouble. Damp cloth only, and keep liquid away from the centre.

## Microwave: The Steam Method

The hack: heat a bowl of water with lemon or vinegar in the microwave for a few minutes, let the steam loosen the buildup, then wipe clean.

**This one works well.** The steam softens hardened splatter and makes wiping easy. Use a microwave-safe bowl, heat it for two to three minutes, leave the door closed for another minute or two, then wipe with a cloth.

**What to avoid**: Using steel wool or abrasive scrubbers on the interior. The coating on microwave walls scratches easily and damage there can be a problem over time.

## Blender: The Self-Clean Trick

The hack: fill the blender halfway with warm water and a drop of dish soap, run it for 30 seconds, rinse.

This works for everyday residue. For anything sticky or dried on, soak the jar in warm soapy water first, then use a bottle brush to get into the base around the blade. Don't put the motorized base in water. Wipe it with a damp cloth only.

## Kettle: The Vinegar Descale

The hack: fill the kettle with equal parts white vinegar and water, boil it, let it sit for 30 minutes, rinse well.

This is a standard and effective method. With Toronto's hard water, mineral buildup in kettles happens fast. Do this every month or two and your kettle will last much longer. Rinse it two or three times with plain water before using it again to get rid of any vinegar smell.

## The Broader Point About Viral Hacks

Most of the damage I've seen from cleaning hacks comes from one of three things: too much moisture near electronics, abrasive materials on coated surfaces, or skipping the step where you dry things completely before reassembling.

The hacks that save time are the ones that loosen dirt before you scrub, not the ones that skip scrubbing entirely. Baking soda, white vinegar, hot water, and a good sponge cover most of what you'll face in a kitchen.
