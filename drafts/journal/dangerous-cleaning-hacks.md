Not every cleaning trick that goes viral is worth trying. Some of them are just ineffective. A few are genuinely dangerous. Experts have been saying this for a while now, and the warnings are getting louder.

After 30 years of cleaning homes in Toronto and Mississauga, I've seen what these hacks leave behind. Here are the ones that concern me most.

## Mixing bleach with anything other than water

This keeps coming up because people assume more ingredients mean more cleaning power. They don't.

**Bleach and ammonia**: This combination makes chloramine gas. It irritates your lungs and eyes, and in a poorly ventilated space, it can be genuinely dangerous.

**Bleach and vinegar**: Also a problem. Mixing them produces chlorine gas. Vinegar is acidic. Bleach is alkaline. They don't work together. They work against each other and create fumes in the process.

If you're using bleach, use it diluted in water. That's it.

## The air fryer baking soda trick

There's a TikTok video showing someone filling their air fryer basket with water and baking soda and running it at high heat. Food Republic and Allrecipes both flagged this recently as a real hazard. Pouring water into a hot heating element risks damage to the machine and, in some cases, electric shock.

The right way to clean an air fryer: let it cool completely, remove the basket, wash it in warm soapy water, and wipe the inside of the unit with a damp cloth. That's all it needs.

## Undiluted vinegar on natural stone and grout

Vinegar is useful. I use it regularly. But it's acidic, and acid eats away at grout and natural stone surfaces like marble, limestone, and travertine. I've walked into bathrooms where the grout was visibly pitted from repeated vinegar use.

If you have ceramic tile, diluted vinegar is fine. If you have natural stone, use a pH-neutral cleaner. Read the label on what your stone or tile needs.

## Toothpaste on everything

It shows up as a hack for cleaning grout, shoes, chrome fixtures, and more. Toothpaste contains mild abrasives that can scratch softer surfaces. Chrome fixtures can be dulled by it. It also leaves a residue that's hard to rinse out of grout lines completely.

Warm water and a stiff brush does a better job on grout. For chrome, a damp microfibre cloth is enough for everyday cleaning.

## WD-40 on floors

Some videos suggest WD-40 removes scuff marks on floors, which it does. But it leaves an oily film behind. On hardwood or laminate, that film attracts dirt fast and makes the floor slippery. A rubber eraser does the same job on scuffs without the residue.

## "Slow vacuuming" taken too far

There was a piece recently suggesting you should vacuum very slowly for better results. The core idea isn't wrong. Going too fast means the suction doesn't have time to pull debris from carpet fibres. But taking five minutes per square foot isn't practical, and it doesn't clean any better than a steady, deliberate pace. Two passes over each section, without rushing, is enough.

## The general rule

If a cleaning trick sounds like it will save you time by combining steps or using a product in a way it wasn't meant to be used, slow down before you try it. The products in your cleaning cupboard are formulated to do specific jobs. Using them outside of those jobs creates unpredictable results.
