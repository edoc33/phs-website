Cleaning videos are everywhere right now. Some of the advice is fine. Some of it will ruin your floors, void your appliance warranty, or send fumes into your lungs. After thirty years cleaning homes in Toronto and Mississauga, I've seen the aftermath of a few of these trends up close.

Here are the ones I'd steer clear of.

## Mixing Bleach with Vinegar (or Anything Else)

This one keeps coming back. People see a video claiming the combination is more powerful, and it is. It produces chlorine gas. That's not a cleaning product. That's a health hazard.

Bleach is effective on its own. Vinegar is effective on its own. Mix them and you have something that will irritate your airways, and in a small bathroom with no ventilation, it can be genuinely dangerous. Use one or the other. Never both.

## Undiluted Vinegar on Natural Stone or Grout

Vinegar is acidic. Marble, granite, and limestone are not acid-resistant. Pouring straight vinegar on a natural stone countertop or tile surround to "deep clean" it will etch the surface over time. You won't see the damage right away. You'll see it in a year when the finish is dull and scratched-looking, and no amount of cleaning will fix it.

The same goes for grout. Acidic cleaners break down grout slowly. If you're noticing your grout looking more worn than it should, this could be why.

## Dryer Sheets on Baseboards (and Everywhere Else)

The idea is that dryer sheets repel dust. They do, a little. But they also leave a waxy residue that builds up on surfaces over time. On painted baseboards, that residue attracts more grime than it repels. After a few months you end up with a sticky film that's harder to clean than the original dust.

A barely damp microfibre cloth does the job without the buildup.

## Cleaning Oven Racks with Dishwasher Tablets in the Tub

This is a popular one. You fill your bathtub with hot water, drop in a dishwasher tablet, and soak the oven racks overnight. The racks do come clean. But dishwasher tablets are abrasive and the chemicals in them can damage the finish in a porcelain or acrylic tub. I've seen bathtubs with dull patches and fine scratches from exactly this. If your tub is older or has any existing wear, the risk is higher.

Line the tub with old towels if you're going to try this, or do it in a large plastic bin outside instead.

## Hydrogen Peroxide on Coloured Fabric or Upholstery

Hydrogen peroxide is a mild bleaching agent. On white surfaces, fine. On coloured fabric, upholstery, or coloured grout, it can strip or fade the colour. I've seen people try this on a stained couch cushion and end up with a stain-free patch that's now a different shade than the rest of the cushion.

Test in a hidden spot first. Always.

## The Toothpaste Trick for Everything

Toothpaste is mildly abrasive. It works on things like scuffs on white shoes or water spots on certain metals. But people use it on everything now: scratched screens, car paint, chrome fixtures, wood furniture. On anything with a soft finish or a clear coat, toothpaste will leave fine scratches. Use it only where you'd use a very gentle abrasive, and nowhere else.

## What I'd Say Instead

Most surfaces in your home don't need aggressive products. They need the right product used consistently. Warm water and a good microfibre cloth handle more than people think. For grease, a drop of dish soap. For soap scum, a non-scratch scrub powder. For floors, whatever the manufacturer recommends.

If a video makes it look like a chemical reaction is doing the work, ask what that reaction is doing to the surface underneath.
