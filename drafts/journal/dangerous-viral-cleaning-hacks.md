If a cleaning trick has millions of views, that doesn't mean it works. Some of the most popular ones circulating right now will strip your floors, ruin your grout, or make your house smell like a salad.

Here are the ones I see causing real damage in homes.

---

## Undiluted Vinegar on Stone or Hardwood

Vinegar is cheap and people love it. I understand. But it's acidic, and acid breaks down the finish on hardwood and eats into natural stone like marble, limestone, and granite. One cleaning, you might not notice. Twenty cleanings, the surface looks dull and worn.

If you want a natural cleaner for floors, use a few drops of dish soap in warm water. That's it.

**Hardwood:** Use a barely damp mop. Excess water causes warping, especially in older Toronto homes with solid wood floors.

**Stone counters or tiles:** Use a cleaner made for stone, or just warm water and a soft cloth.

---

## Baking Soda Paste on Grout

This one is all over TikTok. The idea is that baking soda lifts stains from grout lines. It does loosen some surface dirt. But baking soda is mildly abrasive. Used regularly, it scratches the grout surface and makes it more porous. More porous grout holds onto stains faster.

For grout, a stiff brush and a good oxygen-based cleaner works better and doesn't cause long-term damage.

---

## Mixing Bleach and Vinegar

People think two strong cleaners must be better than one. They're not. These two mixed together create chlorine gas. That's not an exaggeration. It irritates your lungs, eyes, and throat. In a small bathroom with no ventilation, it's genuinely dangerous.

Pick one. Rinse the surface in between if you're switching products. Never mix them.

Same goes for bleach and ammonia. Ammonia shows up in a lot of glass cleaners. Check the label.

---

## Using a Magic Eraser on Everything

Magic erasers are melamine foam. They work by acting like very fine sandpaper. On a painted wall with a scuff mark, they're great. On glossy appliances, finished wood, or car interiors, they'll leave a dull, scratched patch.

**Painted walls (flat or matte paint):** Test in a hidden spot first. They often remove paint along with the mark.

**Stainless steel:** Wipe with the grain using a microfibre cloth and a small amount of dish soap. That's all you need.

---

## Slow Vacuuming as a Deep Clean

There's a trend right now saying that vacuuming very slowly pulls more dirt from carpet. The idea has some truth to it if your vacuum has strong suction and you have thick pile carpet. But most of the dirt in carpet sits close to the surface or in the base of the fibres. One slow pass does about the same as two normal passes.

What actually makes a difference: vacuuming more often, not more slowly. High-traffic areas in a family home should be vacuumed at least twice a week in summer. Pets and kids, more than that.

---

## WD-40 on Sticky Residue (Indoors)

WD-40 does remove sticky residue. But it also leaves an oily film on whatever surface you use it on. On a hardwood floor or a fabric chair, that oil attracts dirt and is very difficult to fully remove. You trade one problem for another.

For sticky residue on hard surfaces, rubbing alcohol on a cloth works well and doesn't leave a film. For fabric, a small amount of dish soap and cold water, blotted gently.

---

## The Dishwasher Trick for Everything

Dishwashers have become a catch-all. People put in baseball caps, silicone oven mitts, plastic containers, even shoes. The heat and water pressure in a dishwasher can warp plastic, fade prints, and break down adhesives.

Stick to what the manufacturer says is dishwasher safe. For most things that aren't, a sink with warm soapy water and a few minutes of hand washing is faster than you think.

---

None of this means you have to spend a lot on fancy products. Most good cleaning is warm water, dish soap, a good microfibre cloth, and knowing which surface you're dealing with.
