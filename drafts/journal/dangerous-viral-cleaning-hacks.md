If a cleaning trick is going viral, that usually means it looks good on camera. It doesn't mean it works. And some of them will cause real damage to your home.

Here are the ones worth skipping.

## Mixing Cleaners to "Boost" Power

This one comes up constantly. Bleach and vinegar. Bleach and dish soap. Hydrogen peroxide and vinegar.

Some of these combinations produce chlorine gas. Others just cancel each other out and do nothing. Neither outcome is what you want.

Use one product at a time. Rinse the surface between products if you're switching. That's it.

## Undiluted Vinegar on Natural Stone or Grout

Vinegar is acidic. That's why it cuts soap scum on glass. It's also why it slowly eats away at grout, marble, travertine, and limestone.

I've seen beautiful countertops get etched from someone following a "natural cleaning" video. The damage doesn't show up right away. That's the problem.

On stone, use a pH-neutral cleaner. On grout, use a proper grout cleaner or a baking soda paste, not vinegar.

## Baking Soda Paste on Everything

Baking soda is mildly abrasive. On some surfaces, that's useful. On others, it scratches.

Don't scrub it on stainless steel appliances, glass stovetops, or anything with a polished finish. You'll see fine scratches under the right light, and they don't go away.

## Cleaning Your Air Fryer with Anything Wet and Aggressive

The TikTok air fryer trend has been going around for months. The general idea is to fill the basket with soapy water and run it through a cycle.

Most manufacturers specifically warn against this. It can damage the heating element and the non-stick coating. Once the coating starts to break down, you've got a food safety issue.

Unplug it. Let it cool. Wipe the basket with a damp cloth and mild dish soap. Use a soft brush for stuck bits. That's all it needs.

## Dryer Sheets on Wood Floors

Some videos suggest rubbing a dryer sheet along hardwood to pick up dust and add shine. It does pick up dust. But it also leaves a thin waxy residue that builds up over time and dulls the finish.

Stick to a dry microfibre mop for hardwood. If you need something slightly damp, wring it out almost completely first.

## Toilet Tank Tablets with Bleach

Drop-in bleach tablets are popular because they're easy. The problem is that bleach sitting in the tank all the time corrodes the rubber flappers and seals. Repairs and replacements add up.

If you want a clean toilet tank, drain it and scrub it manually every few months. Takes ten minutes.

## The "Slow Vacuuming" Trend

There's a viral idea going around that vacuuming slowly picks up more dirt. The logic makes some sense. But the actual difference depends far more on your vacuum's suction, the brush roll, and whether your filters are clean.

A slow pass with a clogged filter does very little. Clean your filter and empty the canister regularly. That matters more than your speed.

---

Not every hack is dangerous. Some are genuinely useful. But "it went viral" is not the same as "it works" or "it's safe." When in doubt, check what the manufacturer says about the surface or appliance you're cleaning.
