Wildfire smoke doesn't stay outside. It seeps in through window gaps, HVAC returns, and open doors, and it settles into fabric, walls, and flooring. If your home smelled smoky during a recent air quality alert, there's residue in there even after the smell fades.

Here's what to clean, and in what order.

## Start with air circulation, but only when it's safe

Before you open anything, check the air quality index for Toronto. When it drops back to a safe level, open windows and run fans to push stale air out. Until then, keep windows closed and run your HVAC on recirculate if you have that option.

## Change your furnace filter right away

This is the most important step most people skip. Your filter caught a lot of that smoke. If it stays in, your system keeps pushing contaminated air through the house. Replace it, even if you changed it recently.

## Clean soft surfaces first

Smoke particles bind to fabric. That means curtains, couch cushions, throw blankets, pillows, and area rugs all need attention.

- **Curtains and washable fabric**: Run them through the washing machine with a cup of white vinegar added to the cycle.
- **Upholstered furniture**: Sprinkle baking soda on cushions, let it sit for 30 minutes, then vacuum thoroughly.
- **Area rugs**: Vacuum both sides. If the smell is strong, take them outside to air out on a dry day.

## Wipe down hard surfaces

Smoke leaves a fine oily film on walls, counters, and shelves. You can feel it if you run a finger along a wall after a heavy smoke event.

- **Walls and ceilings**: Wipe with a solution of warm water and a few drops of dish soap. Work from top to bottom. Rinse with a clean damp cloth.
- **Counters and hard furniture**: The same warm soapy water works fine. Pay attention to horizontal surfaces where particles settle.
- **Windows and glass**: Clean with white vinegar and water. Smoke film is especially visible on glass once it dries.

## Don't forget these spots

- **Blinds**: Wipe each slat with a damp cloth. Smoke collects there and most people miss it.
- **Light fixtures and ceiling fans**: Dust and smoke residue mix up there. Wipe the blades and the fixture housing.
- **Air vents and registers**: Unscrew the covers and wash them in the sink. Wipe inside the duct opening as far as you can reach.

## What about the smell that won't leave?

If you've cleaned everything and the smell is still there, it's usually in the walls or deep in carpet fibres. Activated charcoal bowls placed around the room help absorb odour over several days. A bowl of white vinegar left out overnight does the same thing, though it smells sharp while it's working.

If the smoke event was severe, the film on your walls may need more than a wipe-down. Some situations call for washing with a TSP substitute (available at most hardware stores), especially on ceilings.

## When to call someone

One smoke event that lasted a day or two is manageable with what's above. But if your home was exposed over several days, or if you have older plaster walls that absorbed a lot, it may be worth having someone come in and do a thorough clean. Smoke residue can cause ongoing respiratory irritation, especially for kids or anyone with asthma.
