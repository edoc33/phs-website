Wildfire smoke is not the same as cigarette smoke or a burnt pan. It travels farther, carries more particles, and gets into parts of your home you'd never think to clean. If the air quality warnings have been firing off on your phone this summer, your home likely has residue you can't see yet.

Here's what's actually happening and what to do about it.

## Why Smoke Is Harder to Clean Than You Think

Smoke particles are tiny. They pass through window screens, slip through door gaps, and get pulled into your home through your HVAC system every time it runs. Once inside, those particles settle on walls, ceilings, fabric, and inside your air ducts. The smell you notice is just the easy part. The film left behind is what causes long-term damage if you ignore it.

## What to Clean First

**Soft furnishings**: Curtains, throw blankets, couch cushion covers, and pillow covers trap smoke particles fast. Wash anything removable on a hot cycle. For items that can't be washed, a thorough vacuuming with a HEPA filter vacuum helps.

**Hard surfaces**: Walls and ceilings collect a thin film of smoke residue. Wipe them down with a damp cloth and a small amount of dish soap mixed in warm water. Work from top to bottom so you're not dripping dirty water onto surfaces you've already cleaned.

**Windows and sills**: These tend to get overlooked. Smoke residue builds up on glass and the sills around frames. Wipe down with a glass cleaner and a dry cloth.

**Floors**: Smoke particles that settle in the air eventually land on your floors. Vacuum before you mop. If you mop first, you're just pushing particles around.

**Mattresses and upholstered furniture**: Sprinkle baking soda, let it sit for a few hours, then vacuum it up. It won't eliminate a heavy smell, but it helps with mild exposure.

## The Part Most People Miss

Your HVAC filter. This is where a lot of the damage sits after a wildfire smoke event. If your system was running during a smoke advisory, your filter has likely caught a heavy load of particles. Change it now. If your ductwork smells musty or smoky, a professional duct cleaning is worth considering before you run your heat this fall.

Also: the inside of your fridge vents, your bathroom exhaust fans, and your range hood filter. These are all air movers that pull particles through them. Wipe down the visible parts and rinse or replace filters where possible.

## When the Smell Won't Leave

If you've cleaned everything and the smell is still there after a week, it's likely sitting in a material you haven't reached yet. Common culprits are carpets, fabric sofas, and older insulation inside walls. At that point, an ozone treatment or professional deodorizing service may be your next step.

Covering the smell with candles or air freshener won't fix it. It just layers scents on top of particles that are still there.

## A Few Practical Notes for Toronto Homes

Older Toronto homes with plaster walls, original wood trim, and older insulation tend to absorb and hold smoke odour longer than newer builds. The materials are more porous. If your home is pre-1980s and you noticed a strong smoke smell during this summer's air quality events, it's worth being thorough rather than waiting to see if it fades.

Condos are not protected just because you're on a higher floor. Shared ventilation systems can carry smoke between units.
