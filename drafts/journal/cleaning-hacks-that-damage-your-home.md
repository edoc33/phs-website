Some cleaning hacks spread fast online. That doesn't mean they work. A few of them will cost you money in repairs, or land you at the doctor.

Here are the ones I see come up most often, and why I'd leave them alone.

## Baking Soda and Vinegar on Everything

People treat this combination like a miracle cleaner. It fizzes, so it feels like it's doing something. What it's actually doing is neutralizing itself. The acid and the base cancel each other out. What you're left with is mostly water.

Worse, vinegar is acidic. Use it on natural stone, grout, or hardwood and you'll etch or dull the surface over time. I've seen countertops stripped of their finish this way.

Use vinegar on glass. Use baking soda as a gentle scrub on porcelain or enamel. Don't mix them and expect results.

## Baking Soda in the Oven to "Steam Clean"

Pouring baking soda on the bottom of your oven and running it on low heat does very little for baked-on grease. The residue can also get into the heating element. That's a repair call you don't need.

For oven cleaning, a paste of baking soda and water left overnight, then scrubbed off, works fine. That's a different thing entirely.

## Dish Soap in the Washing Machine

Dish soap and laundry detergent are not the same product. Dish soap produces much more foam. One capful in a front-loader and you'll have suds spilling out of the door. It can damage the gaskets and clog the drain pump.

If you're out of laundry detergent, run a short cycle with nothing. It's better than the alternative.

## The Air Fryer Water-and-Soap Trick

This one has been going around on TikTok. The idea is to put soapy water in the air fryer basket and run it to "steam clean" the inside. Some versions tell you to add dish soap and vinegar together.

Heating soapy water in an appliance designed for dry heat can damage the non-stick coating. Running vinegar and soap together produces fumes that shouldn't be inhaled in a small kitchen. A few cleaning experts have flagged this one as genuinely hazardous.

The right way to clean an air fryer: unplug it, remove the basket, soak it in warm soapy water, and wipe the inside of the unit with a damp cloth. That's it.

## Lemon Juice on Cutting Boards

Lemon juice smells fresh. It does have some antibacterial properties. But it's not strong enough to properly disinfect a cutting board that's had raw meat on it, and it won't penetrate wood grain where bacteria actually live.

Wash wooden boards with hot soapy water and let them dry fully upright. Oil them occasionally with food-grade mineral oil to prevent cracking. Don't rely on citrus as your main line of defense.

## Dryer Sheets on Baseboards

This one's harmless but overhyped. The idea is that the anti-static coating repels dust. In practice, the effect is minimal and wears off quickly. You'd go through a lot of dryer sheets to cover a whole home.

A damp cloth or microfibre cloth along the baseboard does the same job. It's also free.

## Hydrogen Peroxide and Dish Soap Mixed Together

This combination shows up on a lot of "DIY cleaner" lists. The problem is that hydrogen peroxide breaks down quickly when exposed to light and other substances. Mixing it with dish soap speeds that process up. By the time you use it, the peroxide has lost most of its strength.

If you want to use hydrogen peroxide on grout or tile, apply it on its own from the bottle. Let it sit. Then scrub.

---

A lot of these hacks come from people who mean well. The fizzing and the smells make it feel like something powerful is happening. But cleaning is mostly about the right product, the right surface, and enough time to let it work.

If you're not sure what's safe to use on a surface in your home, it's worth asking before you try something new. Some finishes can't be undone.
