If you've tried a cleaning hack and something looked worse after, you're not alone. Some of the most-shared tricks online work fine on camera and cause problems in real homes.

Here are the ones I see cause the most damage.

## Putting a Fan in a Bag of Water

This one spread fast. The idea is that you put a clip fan inside a plastic bag, let it spin, and the bag catches all the dust. Some people reported it broke their fan.

Even if it doesn't break anything, fans need proper cleaning. Wipe the blades down with a damp cloth. If the grill comes off, remove it and wash it in the sink. It takes ten minutes and it's done right.

## Cleaning Grout with Toilet Bowl Cleaner

Toilet bowl cleaner is acidic. It's made for a porcelain bowl, not for the grout between your floor tiles. Using it on grout can eat away at the material over time, especially in older homes where the grout is already worn.

For grout, a paste of baking soda and water works well. Scrub with an old toothbrush. If it's badly stained, oxygen bleach mixed with water is safer and still effective.

## Pouring Boiling Water Down a Slow Drain

People do this to clear a clog. In a metal pipe, it's usually fine. But many homes in Toronto and Mississauga, especially condos and older builds, have PVC pipes. Boiling water can soften or warp PVC over time.

Use hot tap water instead. For a slow drain, a mix of baking soda followed by white vinegar, then a flush with hot tap water, does the job without the risk.

## Cleaning Stainless Steel Appliances with Steel Wool

Steel wool scratches stainless steel. I know it sounds obvious, but the reasoning online is usually "it's steel on steel, so it's fine." It's not. Those scratches collect grease and grime and become very difficult to clean later.

Use a microfibre cloth with a small amount of dish soap, wiping in the direction of the grain. For stubborn marks, a drop of baby oil on a cloth will buff them out and leave a light protective coating.

## Using Vinegar on Natural Stone

Vinegar is acidic. Marble, travertine, and limestone are sensitive to acid. One wipe usually won't destroy anything, but repeated cleaning with vinegar will dull the surface and eventually etch the stone.

If you have natural stone countertops or floors, use warm water and a small amount of pH-neutral dish soap. Dry it right away so water doesn't sit.

## Spraying Cleaner Directly onto Screens and Electronics

Screens, thermostats, and control panels on appliances have gaps around the edges. Liquid sprayed directly on them can seep in and cause electrical damage.

Spray your cloth, not the surface. Wring the cloth out so it's barely damp. Then wipe.

## A Note on Air Fryer Hacks

There are a few videos circulating about soaking the air fryer basket in certain cleaning agents or running the fryer with cleaning products inside. Some of these are genuinely dangerous.

The safe method: let it cool completely, wash the basket with warm soapy water, and wipe the inside of the unit with a damp cloth. If there's baked-on grease, a baking soda paste left for 20 minutes will loosen it.
