Cleaning hacks spread fast online. Some of them work. A fair number of them cause damage that costs real money to fix. After thirty years cleaning homes across Toronto and Mississauga, I've seen the results firsthand.

These are the ones I'd tell you to stop doing.

## Vinegar on Natural Stone or Grout

Vinegar is acidic. That acid eats into marble, travertine, limestone, and grout over time. It won't destroy your countertop in one wipe, but regular use dulls the surface and breaks down the sealant. Once the sealant is gone, the stone stains much more easily.

If you have granite, quartz, or ceramic tile, diluted vinegar is generally fine. If you have marble or any other natural stone, stay away from it entirely. Use warm water and a small amount of dish soap instead.

## Bleach on Coloured Grout or Porous Tile

Bleach is effective on white grout in well-ventilated spaces. It is not a general-purpose tile cleaner. On coloured grout, it strips the colour. On porous or unglazed tile, it soaks in and can cause discolouration that won't come out. I've seen entire bathroom floors lightened unevenly from this.

Read what your tile is made of before you reach for bleach.

## Using a Steam Cleaner on Hardwood Floors

Steam cleaning hardwood floors is a popular hack because it feels thorough and chemical-free. The problem is moisture. Hardwood expands when it gets wet. Repeated steam cleaning causes warping, cupping, and gaps between boards. Some manufacturers will void the floor warranty if you use a steam cleaner on it.

A well-wrung damp mop and a cleaner made for hardwood is all you need.

## Scrubbing with Baking Soda on Soft Surfaces

Baking soda is mildly abrasive. That's useful on some things and damaging on others. I've seen it scratch stainless steel appliances, dull acrylic tub surrounds, and leave a white residue in grout lines that is genuinely difficult to rinse out fully. The TikTok videos always skip the rinse step.

If you're using baking soda as a paste, make sure it's on something that can handle a light abrasive, and rinse thoroughly.

## Putting the Air Fryer Basket in Soapy Water and Leaving It

This one is newer, but I've heard about it enough to mention it. Soaking the air fryer basket sounds sensible. For most baskets it's fine, briefly. But leaving it submerged for hours, especially if it has a non-stick coating, can damage that coating and cause it to flake. Check the manual. Most say hand wash only, and mean it.

## Dryer Sheets on Baseboards and Fans

The idea here is that dryer sheets repel dust. They do, slightly, for a short time. The problem is the residue they leave behind. Over time it builds up on baseboards and fan blades and actually attracts more grime. You end up with a sticky, dusty film that's harder to clean than what you started with.

A dry microfibre cloth does the job without the residue.

## The Bigger Issue

Most cleaning hacks are filmed in ideal conditions with perfect lighting and no follow-up. You don't see what the floor looks like six months later, or whether the grout held up. You see a before and after filmed the same afternoon.

That's not how cleaning works in real life.
