The couch gets used every day and cleaned almost never. By the time most people think about it, there's a faint smell, a few stains that have set, and a general dullness to the fabric. The good news is that most couches clean up well. You just need to know what you're working with first.

## Check the Tag Before You Do Anything

Every upholstered couch has a cleaning code on the tag, usually under a cushion or on the frame underneath. It tells you what cleaners are safe to use.

**W**: Water-based cleaners are safe. This is the most common.

**S**: Solvent-based cleaners only. Water can cause shrinking or watermarks.

**W/S**: Either type is safe.

**X**: Vacuum only. No liquid cleaners at all.

If you skip this step and use the wrong cleaner, you can permanently damage the fabric. Check the tag. It takes ten seconds.

## Fabric Couches (Code W or W/S)

Start by vacuuming the whole thing. Remove the cushions and vacuum the crevices. You will find things. Pet hair, crumbs, a TV remote.

For a general refresh, mix a small amount of dish soap with warm water until it's sudsy. Use a clean cloth, dip it in the suds (not the water), and work in small sections using a circular motion. Don't soak the fabric. You want to lift the dirt, not push it deeper.

For set stains: a paste of baking soda and a little water left on for 20 minutes, then vacuumed up, handles a lot. For grease, a small amount of cornstarch left on overnight before vacuuming draws the oil out.

After cleaning, open a window and let it air dry fully before putting the cushions back. Sitting on a damp couch sets new stains faster.

## Microfibre Couches

Microfibre is popular because it's durable and soft, but it shows water marks easily. Use rubbing alcohol instead of water-based cleaners. Put it in a spray bottle, spray a section lightly, and scrub with a white sponge or brush in circular motions. It dries fast and doesn't leave rings. Once dry, fluff the nap back up with a soft brush.

## Leather Couches

Vacuum first, especially in the seams. Then wipe with a slightly damp cloth. For a deeper clean, a small amount of mild dish soap on a damp cloth works well. Wipe it down, then go over it again with a clean damp cloth to remove any soap residue.

Dry it right away. Don't let water sit on leather.

Once or twice a year, use a leather conditioner. Leather dries out over time and starts to crack. Conditioning it takes five minutes and adds years to the life of the couch.

## The Smell Problem

If the couch smells but doesn't look dirty, baking soda is your best option. Sprinkle it generously over the whole surface, leave it for at least an hour (overnight is better), then vacuum it all off. It pulls odours out of the fabric without any moisture.

Pet smell, in particular, comes back if you just mask it. You have to pull it out. Baking soda does this better than any spray.

## What Doesn't Work

**Steam cleaning at home**: Handheld steamers can work, but only if you know what you're doing. Too much moisture in the wrong fabric causes mould inside the cushions. Most people don't realize it's happened until the smell starts weeks later.

**Soaking cushion covers in the wash**: Check the label first. Many cushion covers shrink or the dye bleeds. If they're machine washable, use cold water and air dry.

## How Often

A quick vacuum every week or two keeps the buildup from getting ahead of you. A full clean two or three times a year is enough for most households. If you have kids or pets, bump that up to four times.
