Most people hire a cleaner and think about what they'll come home to. That's fair. But if you've ever wondered what your cleaner's day actually looks like, especially in a Toronto condo building, it's a different picture than most people imagine.

I've been cleaning homes in this city for over 30 years. Condos have been a big part of that, and they come with their own set of complications.

## Getting Into the Building Takes Time

In a house, you park and walk to the door. In a condo, you might wait for visitor parking, buzz up, wait for the elevator, and navigate a building you've never been in before. In a big Mississauga or downtown Toronto tower, that alone can take 15 to 20 minutes.

When a client books a two-hour clean, that travel and building time matters. A cleaner who shows up at 10:00 and leaves at 12:00 isn't slacking if the unit is smaller than expected. Some of that window is just the building.

## Elevators Are a Real Constraint

You can't carry a full cleaning kit up 22 floors on foot. You wait for the elevator with everyone else. During building-move days, freight elevators get booked out. During busy mornings, you wait.

I've had days where elevator time ate up 20 minutes of a booking. It's not anyone's fault. It's just a condo.

## The Units Are Smaller, But Not Always Simpler

A 600-square-foot condo can take longer to clean than a 1,200-square-foot house. The surfaces are more concentrated. Kitchens and bathrooms are close together and often heavily used. Ventilation is limited, which means smells and moisture build up faster.

Bathrooms in condos tend to need more attention per square foot. A house bathroom gets used by a few people. A condo bathroom might only serve one person, but if that person is cooking, exercising, and living in a small space, it shows.

## Building Rules Affect How We Work

Some condo boards have rules about cleaning products. Others have service elevator windows. Some buildings don't allow exterior window cleaning above a certain floor at all. A few have rules about noise that affect when we can run equipment.

None of these are things a client thinks about when they book a clean. But a good cleaner knows to ask, or finds out quickly.

## Respect Goes Both Ways

There's been some public conversation lately about how condo cleaners in Toronto are treated. Long waits at the service entrance, last-minute changes, residents who don't acknowledge the person cleaning their home.

I'll be straightforward about this. The people who clean your home are skilled workers. They know your space, they handle your personal belongings carefully, and they show up regardless of traffic or weather. Being treated as invisible takes a toll over time.

Most of our clients are wonderful. But if you're new to hiring a cleaner, especially in a condo, a simple hello and a clear walk-through of what you need goes a long way.

## What Makes a Condo Clean Go Well

**Clear access**: Leave the parking pass out. Let the concierge know someone is coming. Note which elevator to use.

**A realistic scope**: A full deep clean of a condo takes time. A maintenance clean of a tidy unit is faster. Know which one you're booking.

**Good communication**: If something came up, the bathrooms need extra attention, or a surface is new and delicate, say so. Cleaners aren't guessing. They're working with what they have.

**Booking the same cleaner**: Familiarity matters. A cleaner who knows your unit works faster and more carefully than someone walking in for the first time.
