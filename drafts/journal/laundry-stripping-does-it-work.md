If you've seen the videos, you know the drill. Towels or sheets sit in a bathtub of hot water, and within an hour the water turns a dark, murky brown. People are horrified. They strip everything in the house.

Here's what's actually happening, and whether it's worth your time.

## What laundry stripping is

Laundry stripping is a deep-soaking method meant to remove residue that builds up in fabric over time. That includes detergent buildup, fabric softener residue, body oils, hard water minerals, and anything else that regular washing leaves behind.

The standard recipe uses a bathtub of very hot water with:

**Borax**: Helps soften water and lift mineral deposits.

**Washing soda (sodium carbonate)**: Cuts through grease and residue.

**Powdered laundry detergent**: Handles the actual cleaning.

Items soak for four to six hours, stirred occasionally, then go through a regular wash cycle with no extra detergent.

## Does it work

Yes, for the right things. Towels that feel waxy or less absorbent than they used to, bedding that smells stale even after washing, gym clothes that still smell after a wash cycle. These are all situations where stripping can make a real difference.

The dark water is real. It is residue coming out of the fabric. It can be surprising how much builds up, especially in items washed with heavy fabric softener over years.

## When it's not worth it

Laundry stripping is not a substitute for regular washing. If your laundry routine is already solid, you don't need to strip often. Once or twice a year for towels and bedding is plenty for most households.

It also does not work well on:

**Delicates, wool, or silk**: Hot water and borax will damage these.

**Dark or bright colours**: There's a real risk of fading.

**Items that cannot tolerate heat**: Check the care label first.

**Already-clean laundry**: If your towels are absorbent and your sheets smell fresh, there's nothing to strip.

## The hard water factor

In the GTA, hard water is common. Hard water leaves mineral deposits in fabric over time, and those deposits are part of what makes towels feel stiff or rough. Stripping can help with this. So can switching to a liquid detergent, using less of it, and skipping fabric softener altogether. Fabric softener is one of the biggest contributors to buildup, and most people use far too much.

## What a professional cleaner thinks

I've been cleaning homes for over 30 years. Laundry stripping is one of the few viral cleaning trends I'd actually tell people to try, with the right items.

The mistake people make is stripping everything after watching one video. Then they're disappointed when their dark jeans come out faded or their delicate pillowcases are ruined.

Do it on white or light towels, cotton bedding, and workout clothes. Be patient. Use hot water. Don't add more detergent than the recipe calls for.

It's a bit of a project. But on the right items, it genuinely works.

## A few practical tips

**Use your bathtub**: It holds enough water and makes stirring easier than a bucket.

**Stir every 30 to 60 minutes**: This keeps the water moving and the residue lifting.

**Don't skip the rinse cycle**: Run the items through the machine after soaking with no added products.

**Don't strip new items**: Stripping is for things that have been washed many times. New towels just need a few regular washes to break in.
