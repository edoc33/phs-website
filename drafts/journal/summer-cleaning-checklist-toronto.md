Spring cleaning gets all the attention. So does fall. But summer in Toronto creates its own set of messes, and most of them build up quietly until you notice them all at once in September.

Here's what's worth tackling now.

---

## Window Screens

You opened the windows in May and probably haven't thought about the screens since. Every screen in your house is now coated with a mix of pollen, city dust, and dead insects. When it rains, that film washes down onto your windowsill and sometimes into the house.

Take the screens out one at a time. Lay them flat outside or in a bathtub. Scrub them gently with warm soapy water and a soft brush. Rinse and let them dry fully before putting them back. It takes about ten minutes a screen.

---

## Air Conditioner Vents and Filters

Central AC or a window unit, the filter is probably overdue. A clogged filter makes your unit work harder and pushes dusty air through the house. In summer, when the system runs every day, filters get dirty fast.

**Central AC:** Check the filter monthly. Replace it if it looks grey. A clean filter costs a few dollars and makes a real difference in air quality.

**Window units:** The filter usually slides out. Rinse it under warm water, let it dry completely, and slide it back in. Do this every three to four weeks in heavy use.

While you're at it, wipe down the vent covers. They collect dust and become a source of it every time the AC kicks on.

---

## Ceiling Fans

Summer is the busiest season for ceiling fans, and the blades are almost certainly dusty. A dusty fan throws particles into the air every time it spins.

Use a pillowcase. Slide it over each blade and pull it back slowly. The dust goes into the case, not onto your furniture. Wipe the motor housing and pull cord with a slightly damp cloth. Done in five minutes.

---

## Under and Behind the Fridge

Summer heat and humidity make the coils at the back or bottom of your fridge work harder. Dust on those coils acts as insulation and forces the motor to run longer. This shortens the life of the fridge and raises your hydro bill.

Pull the fridge out a few feet. Vacuum the coils with a brush attachment. Wipe the floor underneath while you're back there. Push it back. This is worth doing once a year, and summer is a good time.

---

## Entryways and Mudrooms

In summer, the back door gets heavy use. Sandals, bare feet, wet towels from the pool, grocery bags, sports gear. The floor and walls near the entry take a beating.

Scrub the floor properly rather than just mopping over the top. Wipe down the walls at knee height where bags brush past. Check the baseboards for scuff marks. If you have a boot tray, wash it out.

---

## Bathroom Exhaust Fans

Humidity is high in Toronto in July and August. Your bathroom exhaust fan is running more than usual, and the grate is collecting lint, dust, and moisture. A clogged fan doesn't move air properly, which leads to mildew on walls and ceilings.

Turn the fan off at the breaker or switch. Remove the grate cover. Wash it with warm soapy water. Use a vacuum or a soft brush to clean the fan housing inside. Dry everything before replacing the cover.

---

## Outdoor Gear Stored Indoors

Camp chairs, beach bags, pool floats, and sports equipment that comes inside wet or dirty can leave odours and mildew spots on floors and in closets. If something came in wet, it may have already started the problem.

Check the floor or shelf where outdoor gear sits. Look for soft spots in wood, dark patches in corners, or a musty smell. A solution of warm water and a small amount of dish soap handles most surface mildew. Let it dry completely with good air circulation.

---

## Garbage and Recycling Areas

Summer heat accelerates everything that happens in a bin. Even if you take out the bags regularly, the bin itself needs to be scrubbed. Outdoor garbage areas near the back door or in the garage can attract insects and leave lasting odours.

Hose out the bins. Scrub the inside with warm soapy water. Let them dry in the sun before putting in a new bag.

---

You don't have to do all of this in one weekend. Pick two or three things this week and the rest over the next few weeks. By the time fall arrives, you won't be walking into September with a summer's worth of buildup.
