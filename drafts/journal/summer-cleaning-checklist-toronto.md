Spring cleaning gets all the attention. Summer is when the real buildup happens.

Humidity climbs, windows stay open, and people track in sunscreen, dirt, and grass. Here is what to focus on between now and September.

## Bathroom Exhaust Fans

Humidity in Toronto from June through August is no joke. Bathroom exhaust fans work harder during this stretch and collect dust and lint on the vent cover and inside the housing. A clogged fan moves less air, which means moisture stays in the room longer.

Turn off the power at the breaker. Pull the cover off and wash it in warm soapy water. Use a vacuum with a brush attachment on the fan blades inside. Dry everything completely before putting it back. This takes about 15 minutes and makes a real difference.

## Window Screens

Cottonwood from poplar trees lands in May and June, but it packs into screens and stays there all summer. Dust, pollen, and pollution add to it.

Take the screens out, lay them flat on the driveway or grass, and scrub them gently with a soft brush and soapy water. Rinse with a hose. Let them dry fully before putting them back. Screens that are clogged block airflow and push dirty air into the house when a breeze comes through.

## Kitchen Exhaust Hood Filter

People cook more with the windows open in summer, which actually means more grease vapour escaping into the kitchen. The filter in your range hood catches most of it.

Most metal mesh filters come out easily and can go in the dishwasher. If yours are heavily coated, soak them in a sink of very hot water with a generous squirt of dish soap and a quarter cup of baking soda for 20 minutes. Scrub with a soft brush and rinse. Do this every couple of months through the summer.

## Entryway and Mudroom Floors

Summer foot traffic is different from winter. Instead of salt, you are dealing with grass clippings, dirt, sunscreen residue, and whatever comes off sandals and bare feet. That combination gets sticky on tile and hardwood near the door.

A damp mop with warm water and a few drops of dish soap handles most of it. For tile grout that has gone grey or brown, a paste of baking soda and water scrubbed with an old toothbrush and rinsed clean will bring it back. Do not use bleach on grout routinely. It weakens the grout over time.

## Basement and Below-Grade Areas

Basements in older Toronto and Mississauga homes often feel damp in summer even when there is no visible water. That dampness is enough for mold to start on walls, window frames, and stored items.

Run a dehumidifier if you have one and empty it regularly. Check window wells for standing water after rain. Wipe down any visible condensation on pipes or walls. If you notice a musty smell, pull stored items away from the walls and look for dark spots on drywall or concrete. Catching it early is much easier than dealing with it in the fall.

## Ceiling Fans

Ceiling fans run constantly in summer. The blades collect a layer of dust that gets thrown into the room every time the fan runs.

Stand on a step stool, hold a pillowcase over each blade, and slide it off toward you. The dust collects inside the case instead of falling on the floor. Wipe the motor housing and the light fixture if there is one. Takes five minutes.

## Refrigerator Coils

This one is easy to forget because it is out of sight. The condenser coils at the back or underneath the fridge collect dust, and when they are dirty the fridge has to work harder to stay cold. In summer heat, this matters more.

Pull the fridge out from the wall or remove the kick plate at the bottom. Vacuum the coils with a brush attachment. Once a year is enough, and summer is a good time to do it.
