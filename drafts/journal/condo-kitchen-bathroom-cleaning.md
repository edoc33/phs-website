Condos in Toronto and Mississauga are not just smaller versions of houses. They have different ventilation, different surfaces, and different cleaning problems. Grease and moisture behave differently when there's nowhere for them to go.

If you've moved from a house to a condo and felt like things get grimy faster, you're not imagining it.

## Why Condo Kitchens Are Harder

Most condo kitchens have a recirculating range hood, not a vented one. That means the air from cooking gets filtered and pushed back into the kitchen rather than pulled outside. Grease particles land on your cabinets, your backsplash, and your walls faster than in a vented house kitchen.

The fix isn't complicated, but it requires consistency.

**Wipe down cabinet faces weekly**: Grease builds up on the cabinet faces closest to the stove. A damp cloth with a small amount of dish soap handles it when done regularly. Let it go for a month and you're scrubbing.

**Clean the range hood filter monthly**: Pull out the filter and soak it in hot water with dish soap or a degreaser. If your filter is metal mesh, it can go in the dishwasher. A clogged filter makes the problem worse because the hood can't circulate air properly.

**Wipe the stovetop after every use**: This one sounds obvious, but it makes a real difference in a small kitchen. Grease that sits on a stovetop gets baked on the next time you cook. Thirty seconds while the stove is still warm handles it.

**Clean the backsplash weekly**: In a house kitchen with good ventilation, the backsplash might need cleaning every couple of weeks. In a recirculating condo kitchen, it needs attention more often. A mild all-purpose cleaner works fine.

## Why Condo Bathrooms Are Harder

Condo bathrooms are usually small and often have ventilation fans that either don't work well or get ignored. Moisture from the shower has nowhere to go quickly, and mould and mildew can start forming on grout and caulk within weeks if the room stays damp.

**Run the fan during and after every shower**: Leave it running for at least 15 to 20 minutes after you're done. This is the single most effective thing you can do to prevent mould.

**Squeegee the shower walls after each use**: Takes 30 seconds. Removes most of the standing water before it dries into soap scum or feeds mildew. A cheap squeegee hung in the shower is worth it.

**Clean grout every two weeks**: In a well-ventilated house bathroom, monthly grout cleaning is usually enough. In a condo, every two weeks is more realistic. A baking soda paste and an old toothbrush handles surface mildew before it sets in.

**Check the caulk twice a year**: Caulk around the tub and shower is the first place mould takes hold. If it's darkening or pulling away from the wall, it needs to be replaced. This is a repair, not a cleaning task, but catching it early keeps the problem small.

**Dry the floor mat after every shower**: A wet bath mat sitting on a condo bathroom floor is a mould invitation. Hang it over the tub edge or a towel bar to dry between uses.

## Surfaces That Show Up in Condos More Than Houses

Many newer Toronto condos have quartz countertops, large-format porcelain tile, and glass shower enclosures. These surfaces look great but each has specific care needs.

**Quartz countertops**: Wipe spills quickly. Avoid abrasive scrubbers. Don't use vinegar or acidic cleaners on quartz, as they dull the surface over time. A damp cloth with mild dish soap is all you need for daily cleaning.

**Large-format porcelain tile**: The tile itself is easy. The grout lines are the challenge because there's more surface area relative to grout in larger tiles. Keep grout sealed and clean it regularly.

**Glass shower enclosures**: Hard water leaves calcium deposits fast in many GTA buildings. A daily squeegee and a weekly wipe with a 50/50 mix of water and white vinegar keeps the glass clear without harsh chemicals.

## How Often Is Enough

A condo cleaned thoroughly once every two weeks is manageable for most people. The key is not letting grease and moisture sit. A quick daily wipe in the kitchen and a squeegee in the shower between full cleans will make a real difference.
