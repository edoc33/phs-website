Most people clean what they can see. The counter, the toilet, the floor. But after 30 years in other people's homes, I can tell you there are spots that almost nobody touches. And some of them get genuinely grimy.

Here's what gets missed most often.

## The Top of the Refrigerator

It collects grease and dust at the same time. That combination turns into a sticky, dark film that's harder to clean the longer you leave it.

Wipe it down with a degreaser and a microfibre cloth. If it's really built up, let the degreaser sit for a minute before wiping. Do it every couple of months and it stays manageable.

## Cabinet Fronts Near the Stove

Cooking sends grease into the air. That grease lands on the cabinet doors closest to the stove and builds up over time. You might not notice until the surface feels slightly tacky.

A diluted dish soap solution works well here. Wipe with the grain if you have wood cabinets. Dry them right after so moisture doesn't get into the finish.

## Light Switches and Outlet Covers

These get touched constantly and almost never cleaned. They're usually plastic, so a slightly damp cloth with a small amount of all-purpose cleaner is enough. Make sure the cloth is wrung out well so nothing drips behind the plate.

## The Underside of the Toilet Rim

Most people scrub the bowl. Fewer people look underneath the rim, where mineral deposits and bacteria build up out of sight.

Use a toilet brush with an angled head, or fold a piece of paper towel and run it along the underside. A cleaner with some acid content, like a proper toilet bowl cleaner, works better here than all-purpose spray.

## Door Frames and the Tops of Doors

Dust settles on any horizontal surface. The top of an interior door is a good example. It never gets touched, and in older Toronto homes with radiator heat, that dust can get mixed with humidity and stick.

A microfibre duster with an extendable handle makes this quick. Do it when you're doing your baseboards and it adds very little time.

## Window Tracks

When you slide a window open, whatever's in the track gets disturbed. Most tracks are full of dust, dead insects, and debris from outside. In Toronto homes near busy streets, it can be quite a lot.

Use an old toothbrush to loosen the debris, then vacuum it out. A damp cotton swab gets into the corners. It's tedious, but it only takes a few minutes per window.

## The Washing Machine Drum

People assume the machine cleans itself. It doesn't. Detergent residue, fabric softener, and moisture create a film inside the drum and around the rubber seal on front-loaders.

Run an empty hot cycle with a cup of white vinegar or a washing machine cleaner tablet once a month. Wipe the rubber gasket dry after each use. Leave the door open when the machine isn't running so it can air out.

## Behind and Under Appliances

Dust and debris collect behind the fridge, under the stove, and behind the washer and dryer. This isn't something you need to do every week. But pulling these out once or twice a year matters, especially for dryer lint behind the unit, which is a fire risk.
