Air fryers are everywhere now. So is bad advice about cleaning them.

A few trends have been making the rounds online, and at least one of them has cleaning experts genuinely worried. Before you try anything you saw in a 30-second video, here is what you actually need to know.

## The Trend That Is Causing Problems

The most flagged one involves spraying the air fryer basket with oven cleaner, then sealing it in a plastic bag overnight. The idea is that the fumes break down the grease so you do not have to scrub.

The problem is that most air fryer baskets have a non-stick coating. Oven cleaner is caustic. It does not know the difference between baked-on grease and the coating underneath. Once that coating is damaged, it can start to flake off into your food. That is not a cleaning win.

Another version of this uses dishwasher pods dissolved in hot water and left to soak. Same issue. Harsh detergents break down non-stick surfaces over time.

## What Actually Works

Air fryers are not complicated to clean. They just need a little patience.

**Soak the basket**: After cooking, let the basket cool, then fill it with warm water and a small squeeze of dish soap. Let it sit for 10 to 15 minutes. The grease loosens on its own.

**Use a soft brush or sponge**: A non-scratch sponge or a soft bristle brush gets into the mesh without scratching the coating. Skip anything abrasive.

**Wipe the inside of the unit**: Use a damp cloth with a little dish soap. Never submerge the main unit. Never.

**Check the heating element**: Turn the unit upside down and look up at the coil. Grease splatters up there too. A damp cloth, carefully, when the unit is completely cool and unplugged.

**Dry it fully before plugging back in**: This one sounds obvious. People skip it anyway.

## How Often Should You Be Cleaning It

After every use, rinse the basket. A full scrub every three to five uses, depending on what you are cooking. If you are doing wings or anything heavily seasoned, clean it right after.

Letting grease build up makes it smoke. It also makes the next clean harder. A five-minute rinse now saves you a 40-minute scrub later.

## The Broader Problem With Cleaning Hacks

The appeal of hacks is that they promise less work. Some of them do deliver. But appliances with coatings, like air fryers, non-stick pans, and coated baking sheets, are not the right place to experiment. The coatings are doing a job. Once they are gone, they are gone.

When I see a trend that involves sealing chemicals in a bag with a kitchen appliance overnight, I get cautious. That is not a shortcut. That is damage waiting to happen.

The boring answer is still the right one: warm water, dish soap, a soft sponge, and a few minutes. It works every time and it does not wreck anything.
